Hajj and Umrah · Saudi Arabia

A service that became the brand itself

A mobility solution for elderly and disabled pilgrims, in a place where traditional advertising is restricted. The outcome: a single sponsor worth SAR 180M.

180MSAR sponsorship value
1one sponsor for the whole service
01

Background

During Hajj and Umrah, elderly and disabled pilgrims need a safe, comfortable way to move through very large crowds. This service was built specifically for them.

02

The challenge

The environment is tightly regulated and traditional advertising is restricted. No billboards, no campaigns in the form any brand is used to.

So the question was: how do you build a brand and bring in a sponsor where there is no advertising space?

03

The idea

Instead of looking for ad space, we made the service itself the platform: it is the brand, the sponsorship space and the visitor experience.

Visitors meet the brand while actually using the service, and the sponsor appears inside something that matters to people, not inside an ad.

04

What we did

  • Developed the business concept from the start.
  • Built the brand strategy.
  • Set the launch plan.
  • Designed the sponsorship model and took it to sponsors.
05

The result

One sponsor came in at SAR 180M.

My role

I developed the business and brand concept, and secured a SAR 180M sponsor.

The takeaway

When advertising is restricted, the service itself becomes the advertising.

The sponsor's name is confidential under the contract.

Real estate · Lead management

More sales with no extra ad spend

Marketing said the numbers looked good while sales kept missing target. In under 60 days the conversion rate doubled on the same budget.

2×conversion rate
-35%cost per visit
-40%lost leads
01

The problem

Plenty of leads, very few site visits. Marketing believed its job was done, and sales believed the leads were weak.

The easy answer was to buy more traffic. That would only have made the same problem bigger.

02

What the numbers said

  • A property buyer talks to between 3 and 7 developers before deciding.
  • The decision takes 45 to 120 days.
  • More than 60% of leads drop out before they reach a site visit.

So the problem was not the number of leads. It was what happened after a lead came in.

03

The decision

Leave the ad budget exactly as it was, and fix the journey from first message to site visit to close.

04

What we did

  • Scoring every lead by how serious it is.
  • Smart routing of leads to the sales team.
  • Automated follow-up so no lead is forgotten.
  • Faster replies and clear qualifying questions.
  • Coordinated site visits and a clear owner for every stage.
05

The result

  • Conversion rate roughly doubled.
  • Cost per visit fell by more than 35%.
  • Lost leads dropped by more than 40%.
  • Contact rate rose by about 30%.
  • Ad click-through rate went from 0.8% to about 2%.

My role

Rebuilding the customer journey with the sales team.

The takeaway

Before you buy more leads, find out where the ones you already have are going.

All of it in under 60 days, on the same ad budget.

Luxury perfumes · Saudi Arabia

A 12.8 ROAS, and the company was losing money

SAR 1.28M in sales from SAR 100K in ads, and no net profit to show for it. In two months net profit rose 180%.

+180%net profit
2.4profit per SAR of ad spend, up from 0.5
2months only
01

The problem

On paper everything looked excellent: SAR 100K in ads brought SAR 1.28M in sales. A ROAS of 12.8.

Yet the owner was not seeing money at the end of it.

02

What the numbers said

Once we calculated net profit after every cost, each riyal of advertising was returning only half a riyal of profit. A POAS of 0.5.

Sales were high, but a large share came from low-margin products, with shipping, returns and payment fees on top.

03

The decision

Stop measuring by sales and measure by profit. Campaigns get built around margin, not around the ROAS figure.

04

What we did

  • Reviewed every cost: product, shipping, returns and payment.
  • Calculated profitability for each product on its own.
  • Compared pricing against competitors.
  • Restructured campaigns by profit margin and acquisition cost.
  • Reduced returns by improving the customer experience.
05

The result

In two months ROAS came down from 12.8 to 8.2, profit per riyal of ads went from 0.5 to 2.4, and net profit rose 180%.

The ROAS number went down, and the company started making money.

My role

Consultant: I diagnosed the problem and led the restructuring of campaigns and pricing.

The takeaway

ROAS tells you how much you sold. POAS tells you how much you made.

Skincare · Egypt, UAE & Saudi Arabia

From a 1.8 ROAS to 5.2 in 90 days

A 22-product brand spending USD 15K a month with returns that would not move. The budget was not the problem. How it was split was.

5.2×return on ad spend, up from 1.8
$78.5Kmonthly sales, up from $27K
-38%cost per purchase
01

Background

A skincare brand with 22 products selling in Egypt, the UAE and Saudi Arabia. A crowded market with similar prices, and a budget of USD 15K a month.

02

What the numbers said

We found three problems, each with its own fix:

  • 70% of the budget was going to the top of the funnel, with no nurturing of the customer afterwards.
  • All spend sat on one platform, and people actively searching for the product were not being reached.
  • The same ad was shown to everyone, whatever their skin type or behaviour.
03

What we did

1. We re-split the budget: 40% bottom of funnel, 35% middle, 25% top. Conversion rate reached 2.3× and cost per purchase fell 37%.

2. We added Google alongside Meta: Performance Max and Search. Search click-through reached 7.9%, bounce rate fell 45%, and Google’s return overtook Meta’s.

3. We built a creative matrix per segment: customer content and educational videos. Click-through rose 66%, CPM fell 23%, and purchases tripled.

04

The result

  • Return on ad spend from 1.8 to 5.2.
  • Monthly sales from USD 27K to USD 78.5K.
  • Site visits from 42K to 117K.
  • Cost per purchase from USD 22.5 to USD 13.9.
  • Customer messages on Meta from 220 to 940.

My role

I led the strategy and execution with my team.

The takeaway

Before you raise the budget, look at how it is split.

All of these figures within 90 days.

Real estate · Ramadan season · Cairo

We raised the budget in Ramadan while the whole market was cutting

A residential project in East Cairo with an ad budget of about EGP 3.8M, and a CFO who wanted the campaigns stopped. We decided the opposite.

4.2×return on investment, up from 3.1×
+24%total sales
+34%lead-to-visit conversion
01

The problem

A week before Ramadan, cost per lead had reached EGP 420. Lead-to-visit conversion stood at 18% and the close rate at 11%.

The whole market cuts spend in Ramadan, and the question in the meeting was: do we stop the campaigns or cut them in half?

02

What the numbers said

Before deciding, we went back to the data from the previous Ramadan:

  • The number of leads fell 22%.
  • Site-visit attendance rose 31%.
  • The close rate rose 19%.

So fewer people enquire during Ramadan, but the ones who do are more serious.

03

The decision

We raised the budget by 30% instead of cutting it, and changed the whole way of working to fit the month.

04

What we did

  • Ads ran after iftar only.
  • A new sales script suited to Ramadan.
  • Every lead answered within 7 minutes.
  • Leads sorted by seriousness: hot, warm and cold.
  • Ads connected to the CRM and the on-site team.
05

The result

After 30 days:

  • The number of leads fell 14%.
  • Total sales rose 24%.
  • Return on investment went from 3.1× to 4.2×.
  • Lead-to-visit conversion rose 34%.
  • Revenue rose 22%.

My role

Managing the ad budget and leading the decision with sales and operations.

The takeaway

Fewer leads does not mean fewer sales. Ask the data before you cut the budget.

About

To me, marketing isn't a cost. It's an investment, and it has to pay back.

I'm Youssef Khalil. I started in 2010, hands-on in execution, asking myself one question after every campaign: what did the company actually gain from this?

That question is what made me grow. It moved me away from chasing likes and toward the numbers business owners really care about: sales, margin and cost per customer.

Fifteen years on, I've led marketing for brands and entire groups across Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Europe, in industries that have nothing to do with each other: from automotive and luxury watches to hotels, sports, restaurants and healthcare.

What I took from all of it: the rules are the same in every market and every industry, but the details are what win or lose. My job is to get those details right.
