Influencers selling the trip, not the view

A regional travel company hosted influencers at high cost, and the content only showed how beautiful the place was. People engaged and asked, then stopped at the price and the transfer details.

The client’s name is withheld.

01

The problem

The company sells family packages and experiences, with hotel and operator contracts able to serve thousands of bookings. Engagement was high and bookings were weak.

02

What we found

We reviewed customer conversations. The main reasons bookings were lost:

  • The total cost was unclear.
  • It was unclear whether the experience suited children.
  • Dates were hard to coordinate.
03

What we did

  • Separate packages for families, couples and groups, with a clear total price and dates that were actually available.
  • 18 influencers from target markets, each showing the experience that fits their audience: planning, arrival, stay, activities and cost.
  • Publishing spread over four months in line with available capacity.
  • Pages connected to the booking system.
  • A follow-up team for high-value bookings.
  • Results measured after trips took place, not after booking.
04

The result

  • Total campaign cost, including hosting and amplification: about AED 2M.
  • Visits to package pages: 240,000.
  • Confirmed bookings: 4,000.
  • Completed bookings after cancellations: 3,400.
  • Average net booking value: AED 4,200.
  • Revenue attributed to the campaign: about AED 14.3M.

My role

Marketing manager at the agency running the account: I led the diagnosis, strategy and execution.

The takeaway

Good content alone is not enough. It needs a clear product and someone following up the booking.
